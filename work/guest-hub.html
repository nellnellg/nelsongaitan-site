Case study 01 · Haku Sports · 2026

Simplifying event-day operations for staff and volunteers

Event-day staff often juggle multiple tools to check in guests, manage tickets, process onsite sales, and resolve attendee issues. I helped design Guest Hub, a centralized experience that brings guest lookup, check-in, ticket management, and onsite sales into a single workflow.

01
The challenge

Event staff needed better visibility into attendees

Many fundraising events sell multiple tickets in a single purchase. While the system tracked the purchaser, it didn't provide a clear way to identify the actual attendees associated with each ticket.

This created challenges for event-day staff who needed to quickly understand who was attending, manage guest lists, prepare attendees for fundraising activities, and support guests during check-in, especially at galas and auctions where one purchaser could manage tickets for a whole table.

02
Goals & constraints

What I aimed to achieve

Improve attendee visibility

Help organizations understand who is attending an event, not just who purchased tickets.

Support guest management

Associate attendees with tickets and manage guest information before event day.

Streamline event-day operations

Give staff quick access to the information and actions needed during check-in and guest support.

Constraints

Existing ticketing model

The platform was designed around purchasers and tickets rather than attendees.

Event-day efficiency

Staff needed to act quickly in busy, high-volume event environments.

Multiple event types

It had to work across galas, auctions, fundraising events, and other ticketed experiences.

03
Research

Understanding fundraising event operations

Guest Hub wasn't created from scratch. It evolved from an existing ticketing experience as the company expanded its focus within the fundraising space.

Through conversations with Customer Experience teams, observation of fundraising events, and a deeper understanding of auction and gala workflows, we found a recurring challenge: organizations needed visibility into attendees, not just ticket purchasers. That visibility mattered for:

Building guest listsPreparing biddersAssigning paddle numbersCheck-in and support
04
Key decision 1

Shifting from purchaser-centric to attendee-centric workflows

Before

The platform was built around purchasers and tickets. A table of ten looked like one buyer.

After

Attendee assignment lets each ticket belong to a specific guest, not only the purchaser.

WHY IT MATTEREDThis shifted the platform from managing ticket purchasers to managing event attendees, creating the foundation for guest lists, bidder registration, paddle assignment, and event-day operations.
05
Key decision 2

Centralizing guest management into a single experience

Before

Guest information and event-day actions were spread across separate workflows, so helping one guest meant jumping between screens.

After

Guest Hub became one place for guest lookup, ticket management, attendee visibility, and check-in.

WHY IT MATTEREDIt reduced friction and gave staff a single place to manage attendees.
06
Outcome

From managing tickets to managing attendees

Improved attendee visibility

Organizations could identify the attendees on each ticket instead of relying only on purchaser information.

Better event-day operations

Staff got faster access to the guest information needed for check-in and guest support.

Foundation for what came next

Attendee visibility enabled ticket transfers, bidder registration, and fundraising participation.

What I learned

Event staff aren't managing tickets. They're managing people.

Designing for people, not records

The platform managed tickets well, but staff needed to manage attendees. That distinction redefined the experience.

Clarity above all else

Event-day staff work under time pressure. Clear information architecture proved more valuable than adding new functionality.

Small changes, large openings

Improving attendee visibility unlocked guest management, fundraising, and future event experiences.
