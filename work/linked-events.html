Case study 02 · Haku Sports · 2026

Bridging ticketing and fundraising through a unified platform

Fundraising organizations rely on both ticketing and auctions to engage guests and raise donations. They were closely connected in practice, but existed as separate products in the platform. Linked Events connected them, creating one journey for organizations, attendees, and event staff.

At a glance

The problem

Attendees and bidders were managed separately, so staff prepared the same guests twice by hand.

The shift

Let a ticketing event link to a fundraising event, so guests flow from attendee to bidder.

The result

Less manual setup and guests ready to bid before event day.

01
The challenge

When product friction became a retention problem

Organizations use ticketing and auctions together, but the platform treated them as separate workflows. As organizations prepared for events, staff were forced to manually bridge the gap between attendees and fundraising participants.

Separate lists

Attendees and bidders were managed separately.

Manual setup

Event preparation relied on manual setup and coordination.

Scattered info

Guest information was spread across disconnected experiences.

02
Goals & constraints

What I aimed to achieve

Connect ticketing and fundraising

Create a clear relationship between attendees, bidders, and fundraising activities.

Reduce manual preparation

Minimize the admin work needed to prepare guests for fundraising.

Improve event-day readiness

Help organizations prepare attendees before guests arrive.

Constraints

Existing architecture

Ticketing and fundraising already existed as separate products.

Flexible workflows

Organizations use different event structures, bidder setups, and processes.

Shared guest data

Changes had to work across attendee, bidder, paddle number, and payment workflows.

03
Research

Organizations saw one guest journey, the platform saw two products

Through conversations with Customer Experience teams and fundraising organizations, we found that preparing guests for fundraising required work across multiple disconnected experiences.

The platform treated ticketing and fundraising as separate, but organizations viewed them as a single guest journey:

Registration→Ticket→Bidder→Paddle→Fundraising
04
Key decision 1

Linking ticketing and fundraising events

Before

A gala's ticketing event and its auction were two separate events with no relationship.

After

Linked Events lets a ticketing event connect to a fundraising event.

WHY IT MATTEREDIt created a foundation for shared workflows and reduced operational overhead.
05
Key decision 2

Creating a shared attendee-to-bidder journey

Before

Attendees and bidders were managed independently, creating duplicate setup and admin work.

After

Attendees can be prepared for fundraising through connected event workflows.

WHY IT MATTEREDIt reduced manual bidder preparation and improved event-day readiness.
06
Outcome

From separate events to a connected guest journey

Reduced setup

Organizations no longer prepared attendees for fundraising by hand across disconnected events.

Better readiness

Attendees could be prepared for bidding before event day.

A foundation

It set up attendee-to-bidder workflows, payment sharing, paddle assignment, and future fundraising features.

What I learned

Design around customer workflows, not product boundaries.

One event, not two products

Organizations saw ticketing and fundraising as a single event. That distinction redefined how the platform supported them.

Remove setup work

The biggest challenge wasn't new fundraising features. It was eliminating the manual work before event day.

Connections open doors

Linking events created room for bidder preparation, payment sharing, and deeper integrations.
